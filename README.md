# G-HAPO
Generalized History Aware Policy Optimization

Large language models (LLMs) have significantly improved reasoning capabilities through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, yet they frequently generate unnecessarily verbose and computationally expensive explanations. While methods like History Aware Policy Optimization (HAPO) have shown promise in compressing reasoning for small models, their scalability and generalization to non-mathematical domains remain unexplored. Furthermore, blindly optimizing for brevity risks stripping away essential context required for complex logical tasks. To address this, we extend HAPO to the Qwen3-4B-Instruct architecture and introduce a mixed-data tuning strategy that incorporates mathematical, logical (ReClor), and scientific (s1k) reasoning samples. We evaluate these models across five diverse benchmarks to assess the trade-off between efficiency and semantic integrity. Our experiments reveal that while standard math-only HAPO training effectively reduces token usage, it induces mathematical reductionism, causing the model to produce poorly explained chains of thought in textual reasoning tasks. In contrast, our mixed-data model mitigates this through training on a more diverse dataset, achieving the highest average accuracy of 73.6\% (surpassing the baseline's 70.3\%) and demonstrating superior structural clarity in complex argumentation. These results suggest that diverse training signals are essential for developing reasoning models that are both efficient and robust.
